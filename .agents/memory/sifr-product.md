Sifr is an Islamic bookshop based in Kuwait for students first, then general readers and partner organizations. The user requested Arabic by default with RTL, English switching, and translation structure extensible to Turkish and Russian. Its delivery scope includes the whole Arab world, Türkiye and Russia, explicitly including Dagestan and Chechnya as Russian regions.

**Why:** These are explicit product audience and language requirements, not inferred from implementation.

**How to apply:** Preserve bilingual behavior and prioritize student discovery when extending the store.

Partnerships replace the former institutional wholesale section. The user explicitly prohibited mentioning any minimum quantity anywhere. Partner inquiries should encourage contact across bookshops, publishers, educational institutions, mosques/endowments/charities, research/public libraries, and distributors/book fairs.

**Why:** The user expanded the business scope and wants contact-led collaboration, not a quantity-gated quotation flow.

**How to apply:** Never reintroduce minimum-copy requirements or narrow shipping back to the Gulf. Contact details and social links remain visibly temporary until real values are supplied.

Payments must remain explicitly simulated until real gateway integration is separately requested; never collect real card details. The assistant recommends from the catalogue, answers order/shipping questions in the interface language, and refers religious rulings to qualified scholars.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested a demo checkout and an assistant that does not issue fatwas.

**How to apply:** Do not silently enable live charges, fulfillment, or religious advisory behavior.
